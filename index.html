Exploratory Analysis of Simulated World Values Survey Data

This page presents an exploratory analysis of synthetic (simulated) data fabricated to resemble distributions and relationships found in the World Values Survey, Wave 7 (Haerpfer, C., Inglehart, R., Moreno, A., Welzel, C., Kizilova, K., Diez-Medrano, J., Lagos, M., Norris, P., Ponarin, E., & Puranen, B. (Eds.), 2022). No real respondent data is used. Any patterns or findings shown here are illustrative only and should not be cited as real-world results.

Respondents are drawn from five countries spanning different parts of Asia:

  • China
  • India
  • Kazakhstan
  • Singapore
  • Turkey

Cultural Map — Emancipative vs Secular Values

Average position of each country on the classic Inglehart–Welzel cultural map.

China scores highest on secular values (0.40) while India scores lowest (0.26). Singapore leads on emancipative values (0.45), illustrating distinct cultural orientations across these five Asian societies.

Value Fingerprints — Radar Chart

Six normalised measures (0–1 scale) compared across all five countries.

The radar reveals sharply different value profiles: Turkey scores highest on importance of god (0.81) while China scores lowest (0.28). China shows the highest share of interpersonal trust (0.66).

Individual Values — China vs India

A random sample of ~300 respondents per country plotted on secular vs emancipative values. Larger outlined markers show each country's overall average.

Despite different country averages (China: secular 0.40, emancipative 0.39; India: secular 0.26, emancipative 0.38), the individual-level clouds overlap substantially, showing that within-country variation is large relative to between-country differences.
